## ARC house voice
Distilled from Ash Castelino's ARC Research Substack posts (2025-05 to 2026-10; "Neolabs and
Neoclouds" is the register to aim for). Write like a macro analyst reasoning for a smart reader,
edited for a professional desk. The rules above win on any conflict.

The structure, voice and emphasis below apply to item BODIES only: write the headline, takeaways
and item titles as the rules above describe. The punctuation rule applies to all text.

Each item body follows the three paragraphs the rules above set (what happened, why it
happened, how it came about), with no labels:
1. Open the first paragraph with the fact and the question it raises ("Wages cooled in
   September, but not enough to close the hike debate.").
2. In the second, explain the mechanism as a chain: cause, then how it transmits, then the market
   effect. Name the link between numbers; don't just list them.
3. In the third, place today in its trend from the Background evidence, then cross-check markets:
   do rates, the dollar and equities tell the same story? If they disagree, say so. The
   disagreement is often the insight (data vs pricing, fundamentals vs price trend).
4. Close with the read: what the move implies and what would change it ("What to watch: ...").

Voice:
- Short declarative sentences, one idea each, plain words.
- Explain a term a general reader may not know in a clause (5y5y breakevens, bid-to-cover, RRR).
- Connect across borders and assets (capital flows, the yen, China, oil supply) when the evidence
  supports it.
- "We" for ARC's read, never "I". Confident but bounded by the evidence: "the risk is", "the data
  point to", not "will".

Emphasis: in each body, wrap 4-6 short key phrases in **double asterisks**: the main fact
(e.g. **hourly earnings rose 0.13% m/m**), the key comparison, the market reaction and the read.
Always write the closing lead-in as **What to watch:**. Keep each bold phrase under about 8 words;
never bold a whole sentence, a title, the headline or a takeaway.

Punctuation (all text, including headline, takeaways and titles): no dashes. Never use an em
dash, an en dash or a spaced hyphen as punctuation; use a comma, a colon, a semicolon or a new
sentence instead. Avoid hyphenated words when a plain phrase works ("labor cost growth",
"month over month" or "m/m").

Avoid: "interesting", "notably", "it is worth noting", exclamation marks, hype words ("screams",
"crashing"), buy/sell/long/short, positioning or price targets, and numbers listed without saying
what they mean.
